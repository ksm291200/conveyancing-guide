Offer & Qualified Acceptances

how the contract is built — formal letter by formal letter — before it becomes binding

💡 Think of it like this
You're buying a car. The seller says "yes I'll sell it — but I'm removing the sound system and I won't guarantee the engine." You reply "fine on the sound system, but I need a 30-day engine warranty." They come back and accept the timeframe but reject the warranty. You accept. Done. In Scottish conveyancing, that entire back-and-forth happens through formal letters. Until both sides issue a letter with no further qualifications, there is no contract. One ambiguous sentence can leave the contract open — and either party can pull out.
🧠 What It Is & Why It Matters

In Scotland, a property contract is formed through an exchange of formal letters called missives. Unlike England, there is no separate exchange of contracts ceremony. The missives themselves are the contract — built piece by piece, letter by letter. This means the contract is formed gradually, and until the final clean letter is issued, neither party is legally bound.

DocumentWhat it doesSent by
Formal OfferOffers to purchase on the Scottish Standard Clauses (SSCs) at the agreed price, subject to conditions including searches and satisfactory titleBuyer's solicitor → Seller's solicitor
Qualified Acceptance (QA)Accepts the offer but qualifies (amends, deletes, or adds) specific clauses. Each qualification must be expressly identified.Seller's solicitor → Buyer's solicitor
Further QA / Response LetterBuyer accepts some qualifications, rejects others, may add new ones. Can go back and forth several rounds.Either side
Clean Concluding LetterA letter accepting all outstanding matters with no new qualifications. This is the moment missives conclude.Either side
Missives are concluded only when a letter is issued with no outstanding qualifications. Until that moment, either party can withdraw without legal penalty.
Common QualificationWhat it meansHow to respond
Deletion of SSC Clause 5 (guarantees)Seller won't guarantee that services, heating, or built-in appliances are in working order at entryUsually accept — this is standard. Advise client of the risk.
"Subjects sold as seen"No warranties beyond what is expressly stated in the Home ReportUsually accept — standard condition in almost all Scottish sales
Retention clauseBuyer holds back part of the purchase price pending completion of a specific repair or remediationEscalate to case handler — retention amounts and conditions must be precisely drafted
CML Handbook complianceSeller acknowledges buyer's solicitor also acts for the lender under CML Lenders' HandbookAccept — required where there is a mortgage
Planning / building warrant carve-outSeller conditions the sale on outstanding planning permissions or warrants being in place by entryFlag to case handler immediately — could be a significant red flag
Vacant possession clauseSeller confirms property will be delivered free of occupants and their belongings by entryUsually accept — this is standard protection for the buyer
Local authority repair noticeSeller discloses an outstanding notice from the council to carry out repairsEscalate — must be resolved before settlement or budgeted for by buyer

PartyRole at this stage
Buyer's Solicitor (us, on a purchase)Drafts and sends the Formal Offer. Reviews each QA from the seller. Responds to each qualification — accepting, rejecting, or counter-qualifying. Advises the client on the implications of each qualification before responding.
Seller's Solicitor (us, on a sale)Reviews the Formal Offer received. Drafts the QA, amending any SSC clauses not acceptable to the seller. Responds to each further letter from the buyer's solicitor until missives are concluded.

📋 Step by Step — Reviewing a Qualified Acceptance
  1. Open the QA alongside the original Formal Offer and the QA Builder template in Actionstep
  2. Read the QA in full — identify every qualification (usually numbered consecutively in the letter body)
  3. For each qualification: record it in the QA Builder — note which SSC clause it affects, what the seller is proposing, and what the buyer's current position should be
  4. Check each qualification against the SSCs — is this standard wording or an unusual deviation?
  5. Flag any qualification involving: money, title conditions, planning, building warrants, access rights, or anything you haven't seen before — to the case handler before drafting any response
  6. Draft the response letter using the approved template. Each response must be clearly cross-referenced to the qualification it is addressing.
  7. Send draft to fee earner for review and approval before dispatch
  8. Repeat rounds until all qualifications are resolved. Keep the QA Builder updated throughout.
  9. Before issuing the concluding letter: confirm all qualifications are marked as resolved in the QA Builder. No outstanding point should remain.
  10. Confirm in writing that the client has read and approved the Report on Title
  11. Issue the clean concluding letter — this is the moment the contract becomes binding on both parties
  12. File note: "Missives concluded on [date]. [X] qualifications raised and resolved."
📖 Key Concepts — Missives & SSCs
ConceptPlain-English Explanation
Qualified AcceptanceA conditional "yes" — the seller accepts the offer but changes some of the terms. It is not a binding contract.
Scottish Standard Clauses (SSCs)A set of standardised contract conditions used in almost all Scottish residential sales. They cover things like risk, keys, moveables, entry, and warranties. Think of them as the "default rulebook" for the transaction.
Conclusion of MissivesThe moment the contract becomes binding. Happens when a letter is issued with no outstanding qualifications. Cannot be undone without penalties.
Supersession ClauseUsually included in the Disposition — means the missives are superseded (replaced) by the disposition once settlement occurs. Important: any warranty not carried over to the disposition is lost after settlement.
Gazumping / GazunderingIn Scotland, once missives are concluded, neither party can simply pull out or renegotiate price. This is why pre-missive negotiations happen — once concluded, both parties are legally committed.
⚠️ What Can Go Wrong
  • ❌ Concluding before all qualifications are resolved
    If your concluding letter still implicitly references outstanding points, missives are NOT concluded. Read every single letter carefully. A clean concluding letter must contain absolutely no qualifications of its own.
  • ❌ Concluding without the client's written Report on Title confirmation
    Never issue the concluding letter without written client approval — a phone call is not enough. If something goes wrong post-conclusion, you need to demonstrate the client was fully informed before the contract became binding.
  • ❌ Accepting a qualification with hidden cost or title implications
    Some qualifications look standard but carry serious consequences. A "planning carve-out" clause or a retention for an unspecified amount must be escalated. Always ask if you are not certain.
  • ❌ Not tracking qualifications systematically
    Use the QA Builder for every matter. A qualification left un-tracked can technically keep missives open. Tick off each one as resolved — accepted, rejected, or incorporated into a final agreed term.
  • ❌ Sending a response letter without fee earner approval
    All outgoing correspondence forming part of the missives must be approved by the fee earner. Even a minor acceptance letter needs sign-off before it goes out.

Ordering Searches

getting the legal background reports on the property — the foundation of everything that follows

💡 Think of it like this
Buying a used car without checking its MOT history, accident record, or outstanding finance would be reckless. Searches do the same for a property — they reveal who legally owns it, what's registered against it, what the council knows about it, and whether there are any hidden legal problems that could affect the buyer or their lender.
🧠 What It Is & Why It Matters

Searches are formal legal reports ordered from Property Searches Scotland (PSS). On a purchase, they allow us to advise the buyer before they commit to the contract. On a sale, we provide them to the buyer's solicitor so they can carry out their own due diligence. Searches form the factual backbone of every observation we raise — without them, we cannot advise on title.

SearchWhat it tells usAlways order?
Legal Report (PRR)Confirms legal ownership from the Land Register. Reveals any mortgages, standard securities or charges. Shows inhibitions or insolvency notices against the seller.✅ Yes — always
Property Enquiry Certificate (PEC)Local authority records — planning permissions granted, road adoption status, outstanding statutory notices, drainage connectivity, listed building or conservation area status.✅ Yes — always
Title Sheet & Title PlanLegal description of the property. Boundary map from the Land Register. Sections A (description), B (owner), C (securities), D (burdens/conditions). Free when ordered with PRR and PEC.✅ Yes — free with above
Coal Mining ReportWhether the land sits over past or present coal workings. Relevant for subsidence risk. Essential for properties in former mining areas.⚠️ Case by case — check postcode
Environmental SearchFlooding risk, contaminated land, radon, proximity to landfill. Required by some lenders in certain areas.⚠️ Case by case — check lender conditions
Drainage SearchConfirms property is connected to the public sewer network and water supply.⚠️ Sometimes included in PEC — check coverage
⚠️ The Legal Report shows ownership as it stands at the time of the search. If a week passes before observations are sent, consider whether a fresh or updated search is needed — particularly for at-risk sellers.

  2. Click New Search Order → Select Residential
  3. Enter the property address exactly as it appears in the Initial Sales Questionnaire. Or search by postcode and select the correct address from the list.
  4. Enter the reference: JC / [your initials] / [client surname] / [matter number] — for example: JC/KM/PATEL/68890
  5. Select Legal Report and PEC. Title Sheet and Title Plan come free with these two. Add Coal Mining or Environmental search if the postcode area or lender conditions require it.
  6. Review all entered details carefully before submitting — wrong address is a serious error that delays the whole matter
  7. Submit the order. Note the order reference number in your file note.
  8. Log back in when searches are ready — typically 1–5 working days. Set a task to chase if not received within 5 working days.
  9. Download from My Orders / Completed Orders. Save to Matter Docs in Actionstep as: Searches – [Matter Number]

  11. Send to the other side's solicitor by email

📖 Reading the Search Results — Key Things to Look For
DocumentSpecifically look for
Legal Report — Section AIs the seller the registered owner? Do the names match the signed offer? Is there a discrepancy (maiden name, name change, trust)?
Legal Report — ChargesAny standard security (mortgage) registered against the title. This must be discharged at or before settlement. Note the lender name.
Legal Report — InhibitionsAny inhibition against the seller means they may be prohibited from disposing of property. Escalate immediately if present.
Legal Report — Ownership periodNote when the seller registered ownership. Some lenders won't lend if seller has owned less than 6 months. Flag this if relevant.
PEC — PlanningAny outstanding enforcement notices? Any planning conditions that affect use? Any applications for nearby developments affecting amenity?
PEC — RoadsIs the road to the property publicly adopted? If not, who is responsible for maintenance? This is a common observation point.
PEC — Statutory NoticesAny outstanding council notices to carry out works? These pass to the buyer and must be flagged in observations.
⚠️ What Can Go Wrong
  • ❌ Wrong reference format
    Must always be JC/[initials]/[surname]/[matter no]. No variation. Deviating from the firm's format creates traceability problems.
  • ❌ Wrong or incomplete property address
    You've ordered searches on the wrong property. Contact PSS immediately and resubmit. Always double-check the address against the Initial Sales Questionnaire before confirming the order.
  • ❌ Searches received but not sent to both parties
    File note must confirm both (a) client and (b) other side received the searches. Check off both before closing the task.
  • ❌ Seller owned property under 6 months (Section A)
    Some lenders will refuse to release funds where the seller has owned the property for less than 6 months at the date of settlement. Check this date and flag to the case handler if relevant.
  • ❌ Inhibition or insolvency revealed and not escalated
    Any inhibition against the seller must be escalated to the case handler immediately. This is potentially a deal-stopping issue. Do not raise observations or proceed further until the case handler has reviewed it.

Raising Observations

formally querying the title — protecting the buyer before the contract becomes binding

💡 Think of it like this
A building inspector checks a house room by room before you buy it — structure, damp, illegal extensions. Then produces a report of everything needing to be addressed. Observations do the same thing legally. Until all observations are resolved, we will not conclude missives — the contract is not binding. This is your one chance to raise everything before the buyer is locked in.
🧠 What It Is & Why It Matters

After searches and the Home Report are received, we review the full title documentation and raise formal written queries — called observations — to the seller's solicitor. Each observation must be specifically answered before we will agree to conclude missives. They protect the buyer from inheriting problems they did not know about.

DocumentWhat you are checking
Legal ReportOwnership confirmed. No inhibitions or insolvencies. Names match Title Sheet exactly.
PECPlanning permissions granted and no outstanding enforcement. Road adoption status. Outstanding statutory notices.
Title Sheet — Section AProperty description matches the offer and the PEC. Title number is correct.
Title Sheet — Section BCurrent registered owner. Cross-check names with Legal Report. Note ownership period.
Title Sheet — Section CAny mortgages or standard securities registered — must be discharged at or before settlement. Note lender name.
Title Sheet — Section DBurdens — access rights, restrictions on use, maintenance obligations. Advise client on all conditions affecting daily enjoyment.
Title PlanBoundaries confirmed. Property has access to a public road. Cross-check with Google Maps if boundary is unclear.
Home Report — Single SurveyRead in full. Category 3 items must always be raised. Check for alterations, cladding, solar panels, damp, asbestos.
Home Report — EPCCorrect property address. Still valid (EPCs expire after 10 years). Rating noted.
Home Report — Property QuestionnaireFactor details, building insurance arrangements, recent communal works, disclosed alterations — anything that should trigger an observation.
Home Report category ratings: Category 1 — minor issues, usually ignore. Category 2 — use judgment based on severity. Category 3 — ALWAYS raise as an observation AND tell the client directly. Do not assume the seller will volunteer a remedy.
📋 Step by Step — Raising Observations
  1. Read every document in the table above. Read the full Home Report — not just the summary page.
  2. Generate two templates from Actionstep Matter Docs: Observations Sheet and QA Builder
  3. Fill in Sheet 1 — core transaction info: reference, purchaser details, seller details, property address, purchase price, title number
  4. Work through Sheet 2 — Column E "Yes" flags = must raise that observation. Column E "No" = skip unless something else has prompted it.
  5. Edit each selected observation to be specific to this property and transaction. Never leave template placeholder text. Read each observation aloud — does it make sense?
  6. Transfer all finalised observations into Sheet 3 — the final output. Number them, apply consistent formatting, remove any red template text.
  7. Check: does anything in the Property Questionnaire need to be raised that the template didn't catch? Cross-check manually.
  8. Save Sheet 3 as PDF. Upload to Matter Docs as: Observations – [Matter Number]
  9. Send to seller's solicitor by email (see template below). Enable delivery and read receipts in Actionstep.
  10. Do not BCC the client on observations to the seller's solicitor — this is solicitor-to-solicitor correspondence.
  11. File note: "Observations raised on [date]. [X] observations sent. Task set to chase response."

⚠️ What Can Go Wrong
  • ❌ Missing Section C entirely
    If the seller has a standard security registered in Section C and you don't raise it, the seller's solicitor won't know to arrange discharge by settlement. Always check Section C first.
  • ❌ Only reading the Home Report summary
    Category 3 defects, cladding issues, solar panel installations, and damp all appear mid-document. The summary can miss these. Read the full report every time.
  • ❌ EPC expired or wrong address
    If the EPC address doesn't exactly match the property or it's over 10 years old, raise it as an observation. The seller must obtain a new one.
  • ❌ Template placeholder text left in
    Before sending, read every observation carefully. If it references [INSERT DETAIL] or similar — fill it in or remove it. Sending a template placeholder to the other side is embarrassing and unprofessional.
  • ❌ Not raising a Category 3 item
    Every Category 3 item in the Single Survey must be raised — both as an observation to the seller's solicitor AND communicated directly to the client. Document that you have done both.

Report on Title

advising the client on what they are buying — the formal step before the contract becomes binding

💡 Think of it like this
Before you sign any significant contract — a phone contract, a tenancy, a loan agreement — you should understand what you are committing to. The Report on Title is your way of making sure the client understands exactly what they are legally buying: the title conditions, the burdens, the factoring obligations, the outstanding repairs flagged in the Home Report, and any special mortgage conditions. It is the last checkpoint before the contract is binding. Without it, you cannot conclude missives.
🧠 What It Is & Why It Matters

The Report on Title is a formal letter to the client summarising everything legally significant about the property they are about to buy. It is prepared after observations are resolved — but before the concluding letter is sent. The client must read it and provide written confirmation that they are happy to proceed before we issue the concluding letter.

Section of the ReportWhat it coversWhere the info comes from
Property descriptionThe legal description from Title Sheet Section A — exactly what the client is buyingTitle Sheet Section A
Title burdens & conditionsSection D translated into plain English — restrictions on use, maintenance obligations, access rights affecting the propertyTitle Sheet Section D
FactoringWho the factor is, approximate charges, any recent or upcoming communal works, payment obligationsProperty Questionnaire + PEC
Planning mattersAny planning conditions that affect what the client can do with the property (extensions, alterations, use)PEC
How observations were resolvedBrief summary of what was raised and how the seller's solicitor addressed each observationObservations correspondence
LBTTThe calculated amount, whether first-time buyer nil rate applies, whether ADS appliesLBTT calculation
Mortgage conditionsSummary of any special conditions in the mortgage offer that the client must comply with at or after settlementMortgage offer
Recommendation & next stepsClear statement recommending the client confirms in writing if they wish to proceedStandard wording
Non-negotiable: The Report on Title is not optional and cannot be waived. Without written client confirmation of this report, you cannot issue the concluding letter. This is a mandatory step in every purchase matter.

📋 Step by Step — Preparing and Sending the Report on Title
  1. Wait until all observations are resolved and you have the seller's solicitor's formal responses — the RoT should reflect the final resolved position
  2. Generate the Report on Title template from Actionstep Matter Docs
  3. Fill in the property details using Title Sheet Section A — use the exact legal description, not the marketing address
  4. Translate Section D burdens into clear, plain English. Focus on what practically affects the client: restrictions on alterations, maintenance obligations for shared areas, access rights across the garden.
  5. Summarise factoring arrangements: factor name, approximate annual charges, any recent or upcoming works levied on the property
  6. For each significant observation raised: note it briefly and explain how the seller's solicitor addressed it
  7. Insert the LBTT figure. Confirm FTB status and ADS status before including the calculation.
  8. Note any special conditions from the mortgage offer the client must be aware of (e.g. requirement to maintain buildings insurance from date of entry, restrictions on letting)
  9. Close the report with: "Please read this report carefully and confirm by return email that you are happy to proceed"
  10. Send the report to the client by email — use their preferred email address as confirmed in their AML documents
  11. Do not issue the concluding letter until written confirmation is received. Chase the client if no response within 24–48 hours.
  12. File note: "Report on Title sent on [date]. Client confirmation received by email on [date]."
📖 Translating Section D — Common Burdens in Plain English
Legal wording (typical)What it actually means for the client
"The subjects are conveyed with and under the burdens and conditions in the Disposition by [X] in favour of [Y]..."This property comes with existing conditions set when it was previously sold. Check the Title Deeds Schedule for the details.
"Not to use the subjects for any purpose other than as a private dwelling house"No running a business from the property (including Airbnb or commercial activity without consent)
"Not to erect any building or structure without prior written consent of the superiors"Extensions, sheds, outbuildings may require consent — not just planning permission but also title consent
"To maintain the subjects in good order and repair"The owner has a legal obligation to keep the property maintained — not just for their own benefit but as a title condition
"To contribute equally to the maintenance of the common parts"The client will share repair costs for shared areas (roof, stairwell, garden) with other proprietors — this is separate from factoring
"Right of access over [part of property] in favour of [neighbour]"A neighbour has a legal right to cross part of the client's land — this cannot be removed and must be accommodated
⚠️ What Can Go Wrong
  • ❌ Sending the concluding letter before RoT confirmation
    Never conclude missives without written client approval of the Report on Title. If the client later claims they were not informed of a burden or condition, you need evidence that they were told before they were bound.
  • ❌ Copying Section D word-for-word without explanation
    The Report on Title should be readable by a non-lawyer. Legal clauses pasted verbatim are not helpful — translate them into what they mean practically for the client's use and enjoyment of the property.
  • ❌ Getting the LBTT figure wrong
    Double-check the calculation before including it. If the figure is wrong and the client budgets based on it, you may be liable. Confirm FTB and ADS status directly with the client before calculating.
  • ❌ Not chasing client confirmation promptly
    If the DoE is approaching, delay in client confirmation can hold up the whole transaction. Chase by email and phone if you haven't heard within 48 hours.

Responding to Observations

answering the buyer's title queries on behalf of the seller — methodically, completely, and accurately

💡 Think of it like this
The buyer's solicitor has inspected the property's legal paperwork and sent back a list of questions: "What about the right of access in Section D? Is the road adopted? There's a Category 3 item in the Home Report — what will the seller do?" Your job on the sale side is to work through each question methodically, gather the necessary information or documents from the seller, and provide a clear, accurate written response. Every outstanding observation is a potential roadblock to concluding the contract — your responses remove those roadblocks.
🧠 What It Is & Why It Matters

When we act for the seller, we receive observations from the buyer's solicitor shortly after searches are provided. We must respond to each observation in writing, providing either documentation, a legal explanation, or an undertaking. The buyer's solicitor will not recommend their client conclude missives until all observations are satisfied.

Type of ObservationTypical Response Approach
Outstanding standard security (Section C)Confirm the seller's mortgage will be redeemed at settlement from the sale proceeds. Provide solicitor's undertaking to discharge.
Home Report Category 3 itemEither: (a) seller undertakes to carry out and evidence the repair before entry, (b) agree a retention, or (c) confirm the client accepts the position. Obtain the seller's instructions.
Unadopted roadProvide evidence of the access arrangement or road maintenance agreement. If none exists, explain and advise case handler.
Section D burden queryProvide a copy of the deed creating the burden. Explain its origin and practical implications. Confirm whether it is still enforceable.
Planning permission or building warrantProvide the original permission/warrant and completion certificate. Confirm no outstanding conditions. If no completion certificate, obtain from client or advise.
Factor and communal chargesProvide the factoring deed or confirmation of the management company. Confirm arrears position at settlement.
EPC expired or incorrectSeller to obtain a new EPC. Provide updated certificate once received.
⚠️ Never answer an observation without the seller's instructions if the response involves a commitment by the seller — e.g., agreeing to repair, providing an undertaking, or agreeing a retention. Get written instructions first.
📋 Step by Step — Responding to Observations
  1. Log the observations received in Actionstep with the date received. Set a task to respond within a reasonable timeframe (usually 5–7 working days, or sooner if DoE is close).

Mortgage Offer

understanding the lender's instructions and conditions before a penny of their money moves

💡 Think of it like this
When a bank agrees to lend you money to buy a car, they set conditions: the car must be insured, it can't be modified, they want to know if you sell it. A mortgage offer is the same — the lender says "we'll lend you this amount, at this rate, but only if these conditions are met." Our job as the buyer's solicitor is to read every one of those conditions, make sure they can all be satisfied, and certify that to the lender before they release the funds.
🧠 What It Is & Why It Matters

A Mortgage Offer is the formal written offer from the lender to provide mortgage finance for a specific property at specific terms. We act for both the buyer and the lender when there is a mortgage — we must satisfy ourselves that all the lender's conditions are met before we can certify the title to them and request funds.

📋 Step by Step — Reviewing and Acting on the Mortgage Offer

  2. Check the borrower names — do they exactly match the names in the AML documents? Even small discrepancies (middle name, hyphenated surname) must be queried with the lender.
  3. Check the property address against Title Sheet Section A — they must match precisely.
  4. Note the expiry date. If the DoE is close to or after the expiry, contact the client immediately to request an extension from their lender.
  5. Read all special conditions. For each one: can it be satisfied? Who needs to act on it?
  6. Check the CML Lenders' Handbook — Part 1 (general) and Part 2 (lender-specific). Note any specific requirements for this lender in Scotland.
  7. Action all conditions that fall within our remit before settlement (e.g., title conditions, flood searches, structural reports)
  8. Report any title issue that could affect the lender's security to the case handler — even if the buyer is aware of it and wants to proceed
  9. Note the loan amount and drawdown date requirement — the Certificate of Title must be submitted in time for the lender to release funds on DoE
  10. File note: "Mortgage offer reviewed on [date]. Expiry [date]. Special conditions: [list]. All conditions satisfied / outstanding conditions: [list]."
⚠️ What Can Go Wrong
  • ❌ Not checking the expiry date
    If the mortgage offer expires before the Date of Entry, the lender will not release funds. This is a settlement-ending problem. Always check the expiry date when the offer arrives and flag immediately if it's tight.
  • ❌ Missing a special condition
    Special conditions are buried in the offer. Some require a structural report; some require an environmental search; some require the property to be above a certain EPC rating. Missing one means the Certificate of Title cannot be submitted. Read every condition and action each one.
  • ❌ Treating the mortgage offer as the client's document only
    We act for the lender too. Our duty to the lender means we cannot selectively act only in the buyer's interest if there is a conflict. Always escalate to the case handler if a conflict of interest arises.
  • ❌ Wrong loan amount or drawdown details
    When preparing the settlement statement and CoT, use the exact loan amount from the mortgage offer. Any discrepancy will delay release of funds.

Standard Security & MHD

the lender's legal charge over the property — and the declaration that protects occupancy rights

💡 Think of it like this
When the bank lends you money to buy a house, they do not just trust you to repay it — they register a formal legal claim against the property at the Land Register. If you stop paying, they can use this claim to repossess and sell. That legal claim is the Standard Security. The MHD (Matrimonial Homes Declaration) is a separate document that protects the bank from a situation where an unknown spouse or civil partner might have occupancy rights that override the security.
🧠 What It Is & Why It Matters

The Standard Security is the deed by which the buyer grants the lender a security interest over the property in return for the mortgage loan. It is drafted by the lender or their solicitors using a standard form and must be signed by the buyer(s), witnessed, and then registered at the Land Register after settlement.

The Matrimonial Homes Declaration (MHD) addresses a specific risk under the Matrimonial Homes (Family Protection) (Scotland) Act 1981. A spouse or civil partner of the sole owner may have occupancy rights in the property — and if they exist, they could potentially affect the lender's ability to repossess. The MHD is the buyer's signed declaration about their marital status, resolving this issue.

ScenarioDocument required
Sole buyer, never married, no civil partnershipMHD — declaration that they have no spouse/civil partner with occupancy rights
Sole buyer, married or in civil partnership, buying family homeSpousal Consent — spouse/civil partner consents to the standard security
Two buyers (joint purchase)Both sign the Standard Security. MHD or Spousal Consent may still be needed depending on status of each buyer.
Sole buyer, divorced or widowedMHD — confirming current status. Supporting evidence (decree of divorce) may be requested by the lender.
⚠️ Never assume the MHD situation. Ask the client directly: "Are you currently married or in a civil partnership?" Do not rely on what they may have told the lender. This must be established from them directly and documented on the file.
📋 Step by Step — Preparing the Standard Security and MHD
  1. Generate the Standard Security template from Actionstep. These are provided by the lender — use the lender's prescribed form (available via CML Handbook or the lender's portal).
  2. Fill in: borrower's full name(s) as they appear in the mortgage offer, property address from Title Sheet Section A, lender's name exactly, and the loan amount.
  3. Confirm the MHD situation with the client directly. Ask explicitly: "Are you married or in a civil partnership?" Record their response on the file.
  4. If sole and unmarried: prepare the MHD declaration for the client to sign
  5. If married / civil partnership: prepare the Spousal Consent form for the spouse / civil partner to sign. They will need to sign in the presence of a witness.
  6. Send the Standard Security (and MHD/Spousal Consent) to the client to sign. Provide clear signing instructions: sign in the presence of a witness who is over 16, not a party to the deed, and who provides their full name and address.
  7. Receive signed documents back. Check execution: all signatures in the right places, all witnesses have signed and provided their names and addresses, no blanks left.
  8. File note: "Standard Security prepared and sent to client on [date]. Signed copy received on [date]. MHD / Spousal Consent: [state which document and who signed]."
⚠️ What Can Go Wrong
  • ❌ Wrong MHD document used
    Using an MHD when Spousal Consent is needed (or vice versa) is a serious error. The lender will not release funds. Always ask the client directly and record the answer before preparing either document.
  • ❌ Defective execution
    If a signature is missing, the witness hasn't signed, or the witness's name and address are not written in — the document is invalid. The lender will reject it. Check every field before accepting documents back from the client.
  • ❌ Names don't match the mortgage offer
    The name on the Standard Security must match the mortgage offer exactly. Even a missing middle initial can be an issue. Cross-check before sending the document to the client.
  • ❌ Not sending signing instructions
    Clients often don't know they need a witness, or that the witness must be a non-party. Always include a clear explanatory covering letter with the Standard Security.

LBTT — Land and Buildings Transaction Tax

Scotland's property transfer tax — calculate carefully, submit within 30 days of settlement

💡 Think of it like this
LBTT is Scotland's version of England's Stamp Duty. Every time a property changes hands above a certain threshold, the buyer pays a tax to Revenue Scotland. Our job is to calculate it correctly (using banded rates, not a flat percentage), include it in the client's financial advice, and then submit the formal LBTT return through the SETS portal within 30 days of settlement. Miss that deadline and Revenue Scotland issues automatic penalties — there is no grace period.
🧠 What It Is & Why It Matters

Land and Buildings Transaction Tax (LBTT) is a devolved tax administered by Revenue Scotland. It replaced UK Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) in Scotland on 1 April 2015. It applies to residential and commercial property transactions above the nil rate threshold. The return must be submitted and any tax due paid within 30 days of the Date of Entry.

📋 Step by Step — LBTT Calculation and Submission
  1. Confirm first-time buyer status: ask directly — "Have you ever owned property anywhere in the world, including overseas?" Record the answer on the file in writing.
  2. Confirm ADS status: "Do you currently own any other residential property anywhere?" If yes: "Is this purchase replacing your current main residence?" Record the answers.
  3. Calculate LBTT using banded rates — not a flat rate. Do the calculation step by step, band by band. Double-check your arithmetic.
  4. If first-time buyer: apply the increased £175,000 nil rate band instead of the standard £145,000.
  5. If ADS applies: add 8% × total purchase price on top of the standard LBTT figure.
  6. Include the LBTT and ADS figure in the Report on Title and in the settlement statement.
  7. After settlement: log in to the SETS portal (Revenue Scotland's Scottish Electronic Tax System) — within 30 days of the Date of Entry.
  8. Submit the LBTT return: property details, purchaser details, purchase price, tax calculation. Upload supporting documents if required.
  9. Arrange payment of LBTT from client funds held in client account (or confirm it was paid by the client directly).
  10. File note: "LBTT return submitted on [date]. Purchase price: £[X]. LBTT: £[X]. ADS: £[X] or N/A. FTB: Yes/No. Submission reference: [X]."
⚠️ What Can Go Wrong
  • ❌ Missing the 30-day deadline
    This is an automatic penalty. Revenue Scotland monitors all transactions. A nil return must still be filed if no tax is due. Set a task the moment settlement completes. Do not wait until the last day.
  • ❌ Applying a flat rate instead of banded rates
    LBTT is calculated on the slice of price that falls within each band — not as a percentage of the whole. Using a flat rate will produce a significantly wrong figure.
  • ❌ Not confirming FTB status before the Report on Title
    If the client says they are a first-time buyer but have actually owned overseas property, the calculation will be wrong. Ask directly. Record the answer. If in doubt, ask them to confirm in writing by email.
  • ❌ Missing ADS where it applies
    If ADS is missed and the return is submitted without it, Revenue Scotland will assess the underpaid tax and issue a penalty. Always check current ownership status — not just at the time of instruction but again just before submission.

    Certifying something that is not true is a professional misconduct risk. If a condition is still outstanding, do not submit the CoT. The lender will rely on your certificate.
  • ❌ Missing the lender's cutoff time
    If the CoT is submitted after the lender's daily cutoff (which may be as early as 10am), funds will not be released until the next working day — and settlement will fail. Know the cutoff for your lender and plan accordingly.
  • ❌ Submitting without fee earner sign-off
    Non-negotiable. The fee earner must review and approve every CoT before submission. Never send to the lender first and inform the fee earner afterwards.

Redemption Statements

obtaining the exact figure to pay off the seller's mortgage at settlement — date-sensitive and precise

💡 Think of it like this
If you are repaying a credit card, you need today's exact balance — not last month's statement — because interest accrues daily. Redemption statements work the same way. The seller's mortgage does not have a fixed balance — interest accrues every day. The statement tells us the exact amount needed to fully repay the mortgage on a specific date (the Date of Entry). If that date changes, we need a new statement because the figure will be different.
🧠 What It Is & Why It Matters

On a sale where the seller has an outstanding mortgage, we need to repay that mortgage in full from the sale proceeds at settlement. To do this, we obtain a Redemption Statement from the seller's lender — a formal document stating the exact amount required to repay the mortgage on a specified date. If the full amount is not paid, the lender will not release the title to the property, and the buyer cannot register their new ownership.

AspectDetail
What it containsCurrent balance, accrued interest, early repayment charge (if any), total redemption figure for a specified date, and daily interest accrual rate
Requested forThe Date of Entry — the statement is only valid for that date. If the DoE changes, you must request a new statement.
How to requestThrough the lender's online portal (most lenders) or by calling the lender's conveyancer line. You will need the mortgage account number (from the seller) and the solicitor's firm details.
Early Repayment Charge (ERC)If the seller is in a fixed-rate period, the lender may charge a fee for early repayment. This comes out of the seller's proceeds. Flag to the seller so they are not surprised.
Daily interestIf settlement happens a day later than the stated date, additional daily interest accrues. Understand the daily rate so you can calculate any adjustment needed.
📋 Step by Step — Obtaining and Using a Redemption Statement
  1. Identify the seller's lender from Title Sheet Section C (the registered standard security) and confirm with the seller the lender's name and mortgage account number.
  2. Log in to the lender's conveyancer portal (most major lenders have one: Halifax via Lender Exchange, Nationwide via their portal, etc.). If no portal, call the lender's conveyancer helpline.
  3. Request a Redemption Statement for the Date of Entry. Provide the property address, mortgage account number, and the DoE date.
  4. Receive the statement. Note: total redemption amount, whether any early repayment charge applies, and the daily interest rate.
  5. Share the redemption figure with the seller (not the full statement, but the total they need to know) so they are aware of the amount being deducted from their proceeds.
  6. Alert the seller if an early repayment charge is included — they should have been aware of this from their mortgage terms, but some sellers are surprised.
  7. If the Date of Entry changes: request a new redemption statement immediately for the new date. Do not use the old figure.
  8. On settlement day: send the redemption payment to the lender's designated account. Obtain a receipt or confirmation of redemption.
  9. After settlement: confirm the standard security has been discharged. The lender will register a discharge at the Land Register — you can check this once done.
  10. File note: "Redemption statement obtained on [date] for DoE [date]. Total redemption: £[X]. ERC: £[X] or N/A. Redemption payment sent on [date]."
⚠️ What Can Go Wrong
  • ❌ Date of Entry changes but redemption statement is not updated
    Using a redemption figure from a previous date will result in an underpayment (if DoE moves later) or an overpayment. Either way, the lender will flag it. Always request a fresh statement if the DoE changes.
  • ❌ Not accounting for the daily interest rate
    If settlement is delayed even by one day (e.g., due to late funds), additional interest accrues. Know the daily rate from the redemption statement so you can calculate any adjustment on the fly.
  • ❌ Sending funds to the wrong bank account
    The redemption statement will specify the lender's account details. Always use the details on the current statement — not from a previous matter or from memory. Bank details can change.

Advance Notices

protecting the buyer's position at the Land Register for 35 days while documents are prepared

💡 Think of it like this
There is always a gap between the day the buyer receives the keys and the day their ownership is formally registered at the Land Register. During that gap, the property is technically still in the seller's name. An Advance Notice is a flag planted at the Land Register that says: "this property is being transferred — protect the buyer's position." For 35 days from filing, no other deed can be registered against this title that would defeat the buyer's Disposition or the lender's Standard Security. It is the buyer's insurance policy during the most vulnerable period in the whole transaction.
🧠 What It Is & Why It Matters

An Advance Notice (AN) is filed at Registers of Scotland (RoS) before settlement to protect the buyer's position during the period between settlement and registration. It was introduced under the Land Registration etc. (Scotland) Act 2012 and provides a 35-day protection window.

AspectDetail
What it protectsThe Disposition (ownership transfer) and, where there is a mortgage, the Standard Security (lender's charge)
Duration35 calendar days from the date the AN is registered at RoS
EffectDuring the 35-day window, no deed registered against the title can defeat the Disposition or Standard Security named in the AN — even if someone else registers something before those deeds are submitted
Filed byUs — through the RoS Advance Notices portal
When to fileAfter missives are concluded and the DoE is agreed — typically 1–2 weeks before the Date of Entry
CostThere is a fee payable to RoS for filing an Advance Notice
Critical: The Disposition and Standard Security must be submitted for registration at RoS within the 35-day protection window. If registration is not submitted before the AN expires, the protection is lost. Set a task for this the moment the AN is filed.
📋 Step by Step — Filing an Advance Notice
  1. Confirm missives are concluded and the Date of Entry is agreed. Do not file an AN before missives conclude — if the sale falls through, an AN is already on the Register.
  2. Log in to the RoS Advance Notices portal (part of the RoS eFiling system).
  3. Look up the property title number from Title Sheet Section B — do not type it from memory. Copy it precisely. A one-character error will file the AN against the wrong title.
  4. Enter the property address — exactly as it appears in Title Sheet Section A.
  5. Select the deeds to be protected: Disposition (always) and Standard Security (if there is a mortgage).
  6. Enter the granter (seller's name) and grantee (buyer's name) exactly as they appear in the Disposition draft.
  7. Submit the AN. Note the RoS reference number and the exact registration date.
  8. Calculate the expiry date: 35 calendar days from the registration date (not the filing date — they may differ by a day if filed late in the day).
  9. Set a task in Actionstep: "Submit Disposition (and Standard Security) for registration at RoS by [expiry date — 35 days from AN registration]."
  10. File note: "Advance Notice filed at RoS on [date]. Reference: [X]. Deeds protected: Disposition [and Standard Security if applicable]. AN expires [date]. Task set to register by [date]."
⚠️ What Can Go Wrong
  • ❌ Wrong title number
    Filing against the wrong title is a serious error. Always copy the title number directly from Title Sheet Section B. Do not type it from memory or from the offer.
  • ❌ Only protecting the Disposition, not the Standard Security
    If there is a mortgage, both the Disposition AND the Standard Security must be named in the AN. Failure to protect the Standard Security means the lender's charge is vulnerable during the registration gap.
  • ❌ Not setting a task to register within 35 days
    The protection expires automatically. If Disposition and Security are not submitted for registration before the expiry, the protection is lost. This task must be set the moment the AN is filed.
  • ❌ Filing before missives conclude
    Filing an AN before the contract is binding is premature. If the sale falls through, there is an AN on the seller's title that was not properly authorised.

Dispositions

the deed that formally transfers legal ownership from seller to buyer — the most important document in the transaction

💡 Think of it like this
When you buy a car privately, you receive a signed V5C logbook transfer — formal proof of new ownership. For a house, the Disposition is the equivalent: a formally executed legal deed that transfers the property from the seller to the buyer. But unlike a V5C, the Disposition has to be registered at the Land Register of Scotland to be fully effective — until then, the buyer's ownership exists only in contract. Once registered, it is publicly recorded and legally watertight.
🧠 Structure of a Disposition

Templates are in Matter 3469 in Actionstep. Every Disposition follows a standard structure with sections that cannot be altered without legal justification.

SectionWhat it containsCan you edit?
GranterThe seller's full legal name and address — exactly as in the title sheetYes — fill in from title sheet
GranteeThe buyer's full legal name and address — exactly as in the AML documents and mortgage offerYes — fill in from AML docs
ConsiderationThe purchase price stated as "in consideration of the sum of £[X] paid by the Grantee to the Granter"Yes — use agreed price
Property descriptionThe precise legal description from Title Sheet Section A, including the title numberYes — copy exactly from title sheet
WarrandiceThe seller's guarantee of title — usually "absolute warrandice" meaning the seller guarantees they have good title to grantNo — do not alter
Date of EntryThe agreed completion dateYes — insert agreed DoE
Supersession clauseStates that the missives are superseded by the Disposition once settlement occursNo — standard clause
Execution blockSignature of granter (seller) with witness signature, name, and addressNo — signed by seller
📋 Step by Step — Preparing and Checking the Disposition
  1. Generate the Disposition template from Matter 3469 in Actionstep
  2. Fill in: seller's full name and address (from title sheet / AML), buyer's full name and address (from AML and mortgage offer), purchase price, property description copied word-for-word from Title Sheet Section A, title number, and Date of Entry.
  3. Do not alter the warrandice clause or the supersession clause without case handler instruction
  4. Send the draft Disposition to the seller's solicitor (if we act for the buyer) for their review and for the seller to execute
  5. When the executed Disposition comes back from the seller's solicitor: check execution carefully — seller has signed in the right place, witness has signed, witness has printed their full name and address
  6. Confirm: the seller's name on the Disposition matches the registered owner in Title Sheet Section B exactly
  7. Confirm: the buyer's name matches the mortgage offer and AML documents exactly
  8. Confirm: property description matches Title Sheet Section A exactly
  9. If we act for the seller: receive the draft from the buyer's solicitor, review it, have the seller execute it in the presence of a witness, and return it
  10. After settlement: submit the Disposition (and Standard Security) for registration at RoS within the 35-day Advance Notice window
  11. File note: "Disposition prepared / received on [date]. Execution checked. Submitted for registration at RoS on [date]."
⚠️ What Can Go Wrong
  • ❌ Name discrepancy between Disposition and title sheet
    If the seller's name on the Disposition doesn't match the registered owner in the title sheet (e.g., maiden name vs married name), RoS will reject the registration. Flag this before settlement.
  • ❌ Defective execution
    The seller must sign and have the signature witnessed — witness must sign, and print their full name and address. Check all fields are complete before settlement. A defectively executed Disposition cannot be registered.
  • ❌ Wrong property description
    Copy the property description from Title Sheet Section A verbatim. Do not paraphrase or abbreviate. Any discrepancy creates a registration problem.
  • ❌ Not submitting within the Advance Notice window
    The Advance Notice expires after 35 days. Failing to submit within this window loses the buyer's protection. Always check the AN expiry date against your submission date.

Settlement Day

funds move, keys change hands — every step must happen in the right order within a tight window

💡 Think of it like this
Settlement day is like an orchestra performance — every instrument must play in the right order, at the right time, for the whole thing to work. The lender releases funds to us. We release funds to the seller's solicitor. The seller's solicitor releases the keys. The redemption is paid to the lender. A discharge is arranged. If any step is late or out of order, the whole settlement can unravel — and the client is left without keys or without their money. Your job is to make sure every step is set up in advance so the day itself runs smoothly.
🧠 What Happens on Settlement Day

Settlement in Scotland is almost always simultaneous — funds are released and keys change hands on the same day, usually by noon. Unlike English completions, there is no separate "exchange" moment beforehand (missives do that work). By the Date of Entry, all documents should be ready, all funds accounted for, and all checks completed. The settlement statement sets out the money flows clearly.

The settlement statement is prepared before DoE and shared with the client. It shows exactly where every pound goes. On a purchase it looks like this:

📋 Step by Step — Settlement Day (Purchase)
  1. Prepare the settlement statement and send to the client for approval at least 3 working days before DoE. Confirm they understand the amount they need to transfer.
  2. Chase the client's balance funds — ensure they have transferred their contribution to our client account no later than the day before DoE.
  3. Submit the Certificate of Title to the lender — before their daily cutoff — to trigger release of mortgage funds.
  4. On the morning of DoE: confirm mortgage funds have arrived in our client account. Do not proceed until confirmed.
  5. Confirm LISA funds are also in the client account if applicable.
  6. Once all funds are confirmed: send the full purchase price (mortgage + LISA + client balance) by CHAPS transfer to the seller's solicitor's client account.
  7. Email the seller's solicitor: "We have remitted £[X] to your client account in settlement of the above. Please confirm receipt and release of the keys."
  8. Receive confirmation from seller's solicitor that funds are received and keys have been released.
  9. Receive the signed Disposition and Standard Security (if relevant) from the seller's solicitor.
  10. Notify the client: "Settlement has completed. You can collect your keys from [estate agent / seller direct]."
  11. Set tasks immediately: LBTT return (within 30 days), submit Disposition for registration (within Advance Notice window).
  12. File note: "Settlement completed on [date]. Funds sent: £[X] at [time]. Keys released. Disposition received. LBTT task set. Registration task set."
⚠️ What Can Go Wrong
  • ❌ Client hasn't transferred their balance
    If the client's balance hasn't arrived by DoE morning, we cannot send the full purchase price to the seller's solicitor. Chase the client aggressively from 3 days out. Make clear that a same-day CHAPS transfer arriving after 2pm may not be processed in time.
  • ❌ Mortgage funds delayed by the lender
    Occasionally lenders are slow to release. If funds haven't arrived by 10am, call the lender immediately. Have the CoT submission reference ready. If funds are not coming, the seller's solicitor must be notified and settlement may need to be deferred.
  • ❌ Sending funds before receiving confirmation of all money in account
    Never send the purchase price until all funds (mortgage + LISA + client balance) are confirmed in your client account. Sending a shortfall is a serious error — you cannot recall a CHAPS payment.
  • ❌ Forgetting post-settlement tasks
    LBTT must be submitted within 30 days. Registration must be submitted within the Advance Notice window. Set both tasks the moment settlement confirms. Do not rely on memory.

Post-Settlement

LBTT return, registration at RoS, discharge of seller's security, and closing the file properly

💡 Think of it like this
Settlement day feels like the finish line — but it is actually the start of the final lap. The buyer has the keys, but they are not yet the registered owner. The LBTT has not yet been paid. The seller's mortgage has not yet been formally discharged from the register. And the file is not closed. Post-settlement is where all of this is tied up, documented, and finalised. It is less dramatic than settlement day but equally important — missing the 30-day LBTT deadline or the 35-day registration window are the kind of mistakes that result in penalties and professional complaints.

📋 Step by Step — Post-Settlement Tasks (Purchase)
  1. LBTT return: Log in to the SETS portal (Revenue Scotland). Submit the return within 30 days of DoE. Enter the purchase price, buyer details, FTB and ADS status. Pay any tax due from client funds in our client account. Note the submission reference.
  2. Submit for registration: Submit the Disposition and Standard Security to RoS via DPS (Digital Plans Service) or eFiling — within the 35-day Advance Notice window. Attach LBTT receipt to the Disposition registration application.
  3. Confirm registration: RoS will issue a confirmation once the title is updated. This may take several weeks. When received, send a copy of the new title sheet to the client showing them as the registered owner.
  4. Discharge of seller's security (sale side): After redemption funds are received by the seller's lender, the lender will arrange registration of a Discharge. Check this has appeared in the title sheet once registration is processed. Alert the case handler if a discharge hasn't appeared within a reasonable time.
  5. Account to client: Once all funds are reconciled, issue the final financial statement to the client showing all monies received and disbursed. Confirm any balance returned or outstanding.
  6. Close the file: Complete the firm's file closing checklist. Archive all documents. Update Actionstep to show the matter is closed. Retain the file in accordance with the firm's retention policy.
  7. File note: "LBTT return submitted [date], reference [X]. Documents submitted for registration at RoS [date]. Registration confirmed [date — add later]. File closed [date]."

⚠️ What Can Go Wrong
  • ❌ Missing the 30-day LBTT deadline
    Revenue Scotland issues automatic penalties. There is no grace period. Set the task on settlement day and submit as soon as possible — do not leave it until day 29.
  • ❌ Missing the 35-day Advance Notice window for registration
    If the Disposition is not submitted within the AN window, the buyer loses the protection it provided. Any deed registered against the title during that gap could take priority. This is a serious professional failure. Always track the AN expiry date.
  • ❌ Not attaching the LBTT confirmation to the registration application
    RoS will reject or delay the application if the LBTT receipt is missing. Submit the LBTT return first, then immediately proceed to submit the registration application with the receipt attached.
  • ❌ Not chasing the seller's lender discharge
    After redemption is paid, the seller's lender must register a discharge. This is their responsibility, but if it doesn't appear in a reasonable timeframe, chase. An undischarged standard security on the title is a problem for the new owner.
